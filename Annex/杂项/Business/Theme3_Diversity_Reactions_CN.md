## Q1. 好莱坞如何随时间描绘中国、中国人与中国文化？或某一中国元素如何在好莱坞电影中演变？

### English
The interesting thing about Hollywood's China isn't that it got more "accurate" over time — it's that the *function* of China kept changing while the accuracy stayed suspiciously convenient.

Think in three eras. First, the Yellow Peril era — Fu Manchu, Charlie Chan — where China was a menace or a servant, never an equal. Then the "aesthetic" era — *Crouching Tiger*, *Hero*, *Mulan* — where China becomes a beautiful backdrop, all flowing silk and ancient wisdom, but still narrated for a Western gaze. And now a third era: China as *market* — *Mulan* (2020) and *The Great Wall* cast Chinese stars not out of respect but to open a box office.

Here's the perspective I'd push: we keep asking "is the depiction accurate?" — the better question is "**who is this depiction *for*?**" Every era got more technically correct while staying structurally about what the West needed China to be — a threat, a fantasy, or a customer. Even *Mulan*, a story we might call ours, gets refitted as an individualist girl-power fable.

The solution isn't demanding better Western representation. It's that **the most authentic depictions of China will be ones we make and export ourselves** — *Nezha*, *The Wandering Earth* — where the audience isn't a Western one to be pleased. Cultural misunderstanding shrinks not when Hollywood tries harder, but when China stops needing Hollywood's permission to tell its own story.

### 中文
好莱坞里的“中国”有趣之处，不在于它随时间变得更“准确”，而在于中国的*功能*一直在变，而“准确性”却始终可疑地“恰到好处”。

可以分三个时代看。第一个是“黄祸”时代——傅满洲、陈查理——中国要么是威胁、要么是仆从，从不是平等的对手。第二个是“审美”时代——《卧虎藏龙》《英雄》《花木兰》——中国变成美丽的背景板，满是飘逸的丝绸和古老的智慧，但仍然是讲给西方凝视听的。第三个是当下：中国作为*市场*——《花木兰》(2020) 和《长城》请中国明星，不是出于尊重，而是为了打开票房。

我想强调的角度是：我们总在问“这个描绘准不准？”——更好的问题是“**这个描绘是给*谁*看的？**”每个时代在技术上越来越对，但在结构上，始终是在迎合西方需要中国成为的样子——一个威胁、一个幻想、或者一个客户。即便《花木兰》这个本该算“我们的”故事，也被改写成了一部个人主义的女性力量寓言。

解决方案不是要求西方演得更好，而是：**最真实的中国形象，将是我们自己制作、自己输出的作品**——《哪吒》《流浪地球》——那里观众不是一个需要被取悦的西方。文化误解的减少，不靠好莱坞更努力，而靠中国不再需要好莱坞的许可来讲自己的故事。

## Q2. 是什么造成了文化误解？有哪些可能的解决办法——用电影/电视/真实案例说明。

### English
Let me name what I think is the real root cause, because "lack of contact" is the cliché answer and it's only half true.

The deeper cause is that **misunderstanding is often *useful*.** It's cheap. A stereotype is a cognitive shortcut that saves you the effort of knowing a person. In *Crash*, the DA's wife doesn't fear the locksmith because she's ignorant — she fears him because fear is *convenient*; it lets her feel safe without ever testing the belief.

So the solution isn't just "more exposure." Plenty of people have contact and keep their stereotypes — that's the paradox of *Crash*: everyone collides with everyone and almost no one changes. Contact only works under two conditions: **equal status and a shared goal.** Put two groups in a hierarchy, and contact *confirms* the stereotype.

The concrete solution I'd argue for: **structured cooperation, not diversity tourism.** A group project with a shared grade beats a hundred "cultural awareness" posters. Sport, joint tasks, shared stakes. In real life — the EU's Erasmus program changes attitudes far better than any anti-racism campaign, because it forces equal-status cooperation over months.

In short: you don't cure a stereotype by meeting someone. You cure it by *needing* them — by having a goal you can only reach together.

### 中文
我先说我认为的真正根源，因为“缺乏接触”是陈词滥调的答案，而且只说对了一半。

更深的原因在于：**误解往往是*有用的*。**它很省力。刻板印象是一种认知捷径，帮你省去“真正去了解一个人”的功夫。在《撞车》里，检察官的妻子害怕那个锁匠，不是因为她无知，而是因为恐惧*很方便*——它让她不必去检验自己的成见，就能获得安全感。

所以解决办法不只是“多接触”。很多人接触了却仍然保留刻板印象——这正是《撞车》的悖论：人人相互碰撞，却几乎没人改变。接触只有在两个条件下才有效：**平等地位 + 共同目标。**把两个群体放进层级里，接触反而会*坐实*刻板印象。

我主张的具体做法是：**有结构的协作，而不是“多元旅游式”的走动。**一个共享分数的分组作业，胜过一百张“文化意识”海报。体育、共同任务、共同利害。现实里——欧盟的 Erasmus 交流项目改变态度的效果，远胜任何反种族主义宣传，因为它逼着人们在几个月里进行平等地位的协作。

一句话：你不是靠“见一个人”来治愈刻板印象，而是靠*需要*他——靠一个只有一起才能达成的目标。

## Q3. 多元政策对一个国家是好还是坏？为什么？中国呢？

### English
Most of this debate gets stuck because people argue about *values*. I want to reframe it as a question of **what "diversity policy" actually is** — because the answer flips depending on the mechanism.

There are two very different things both called "diversity policy." One is **representation quotas** — you engineer outcomes by group. The other is **anti-discrimination + equal access** — you remove barriers and let outcomes fall where they may. The first can backfire: it can entrench the very categories it claims to dissolve, and it breeds backlash, as we're seeing right now with DEI rollbacks in the US.

So: is it good or bad? My answer — **it's good exactly to the degree it removes barriers, and bad to the degree it manufactures group categories.** The goal should be a society where diversity is a *byproduct* of fairness, not a target someone has to administer forever.

For China — the framing is different. China's diversity is less about race and more about **region, ethnicity, language, and rural-urban divide.** The genuinely effective "diversity policy" there isn't a quota; it's *infrastructure and education* — high-speed rail, poverty alleviation, Mandarin-plus-local-language education. That's inclusion through *mobility and opportunity*, not through counting. The lesson: ask not "is diversity good?" but "is this policy widening access or policing categories?"

### 中文
这场争论多半卡住，是因为大家在争*价值观*。我想换个框架：先问**“多元政策”到底是什么**——因为答案会随机制不同而反转。

有两种截然不同、却都被叫“多元政策”的东西。一种是**代表性配额**——按群体来设计结果。另一种是**反歧视 + 平等机会**——移除壁垒，让结果自然发生。前者可能适得其反：它会固化那些它声称要消解的类别，还会引发反弹——正如美国当下 DEI 的倒退。

所以是好是坏？我的回答是：**它在多大程度上消除壁垒，就在多大程度上是好的；它在多大程度上人为制造群体类别，就在多大程度上是坏的。**目标应该是一个“多元是公平的*副产品*”的社会，而不是一个需要永远被行政管理的指标。

至于中国——框架不一样。中国的多样性，更多不在种族，而在**地域、民族、语言、城乡差距**。那里真正有效的“多元政策”不是配额，而是*基础设施和教育*——高铁、扶贫、普通话加本地方言的双语教育。这是通过*流动与机会*实现的包容，而不是靠计数。教训是：别问“多元好不好”，要问“这项政策是在拓宽机会，还是在监管类别？”

## Q4. 中国与外界沟通有哪些有效方式？你还有什么建议？

### English
The default answer is "better PR, more storytelling." I think that's the trap — because the moment you frame it as *communication*, you've already accepted that the problem is perception. Often it isn't.

Here's my angle: **the most persuasive communication is not messages, it's *demonstrated outcomes that others want to copy*.** Nobody was persuaded by a pamphlet about high-speed rail; they were persuaded by riding it. Nobody believes a press release about e-commerce; they believe a package arriving in three days.

So the effective channels are *lived* ones: students who study abroad and go home, tourists, joint ventures, open-source contributions, Chinese films and games that people consume because they're *good*, not because they're "Chinese culture." *Genshin Impact* and *Black Myth: Wukong* did more for cultural curiosity than a decade of official campaigns — because the entry point was fun, not diplomacy.

What else would I suggest? **Stop broadcasting, start collaborating.** Fund co-productions where Chinese creators are *equal partners*, not sponsors. Invite critics in, don't filter them out — nothing signals confidence like tolerating a hard question. And accept that communication is asymmetric: you'll be judged by your worst export, so quality control at home *is* the communication strategy.

The deepest point: a country communicates best not by talking about itself, but by **making things the world can't ignore.**

### 中文
默认答案是“更好的公关、更多的讲故事”。我认为这正是陷阱——因为一旦你把它框定为*沟通*，你就已经默认“问题出在观感上”。而很多时候并不是。

我的角度是：**最有说服力的沟通不是信息，而是*别人想复制的结果*。**没有人靠一本高铁宣传册被说服，他们是靠亲身乘坐被说服的。没有人相信一篇电商通稿，他们相信三天送达的包裹。

所以有效的渠道是*活出来的*：出国留学又回国的人、游客、合资企业、开源贡献、因为*好*（而不是因为“是中国文化”）才被消费的中国电影和游戏。《原神》和《黑神话：悟空》为文化好奇心做的贡献，超过十年的官方宣传——因为入口是“好玩”，不是“外交”。

还有什么建议？**别再广播，开始协作。**资助那些中国创作者作为*平等伙伴*（而非赞助对象）的合拍项目。把批评者请进来，别过滤掉——没有什么比容忍一个尖锐问题更能展现自信。也要接受沟通是不对称的：你会因为最差的那个出口产品被评判，所以“本土的质量把控”*本身就是*沟通策略。

最深的一点：一个国家最好的沟通，不是谈论自己，而是**做出世界无法忽视的东西。**

## Q5. 研究近年欧盟/美国的难民与非法移民危机。欧盟/美国该拒绝他们入境吗？新来者是否应被强制融入？

### English
Two questions are bundled here, and I want to untangle them because they get conflated.

First — should the EU/US refuse entry? The honest answer is that "refuse entry" is a **decision with a price, not a free choice.** A country can restrict who crosses its border; it can't restrict consequences. Refusing entry doesn't make the pressure disappear — it pushes it to smugglers, to informal labor, to deaths in the Mediterranean. So the real question isn't "refuse or admit" but "**which cost are we willing to own?**" A state that refuses must own the moral and practical cost of fortresses; a state that admits must own the cost of integration.

Second — should newcomers be obliged to integrate? Here I'd push back on the word "obliged." There's a difference between **integration and assimilation.** Obliging adherence to *civic basics* — language, rule of law, gender equality under law — is legitimate; a society does have a right to those. But obliging people to abandon their *identity* — religion, cuisine, dress, mother tongue — isn't integration, it's erasure, and it doesn't even work: it produces a resentful second generation.

The distinction I'd leave you with: **oblige *civic* integration, never *cultural* conformity.** You can require someone to learn the language and follow the law. You cannot require them to stop being who they are. The countries that get this right — Canada, arguably — integrate *faster* precisely because they demand less cultural surrender.

### 中文
这里捆了两个问题，我想把它们拆开，因为它们常被混为一谈。

第一——欧盟/美国该拒绝入境吗？诚实的答案是：“拒绝入境”是一个**有代价的决策，而不是免费的选择。**一个国家能限制谁越过边界，却无法限制后果。拒绝入境并不会让压力消失——它会把压力推给走私者、推给非正规劳动力、推给地中海的死亡。所以真正的问题不是“拒绝还是接纳”，而是“**我们愿意承担哪一种代价？**”拒绝的国家必须承担“筑墙”的道德与实际代价；接纳的国家必须承担融合的代价。

第二——新来者是否应被强制融入？这里我对“强制”这个词有所保留。**融入（integration）和同化（assimilation）是有区别的。**要求遵守*公民基本规范*——语言、法治、法律上的性别平等——是正当的，一个社会确实有权利要求这些。但要求人们放弃自己的*身份*——宗教、饮食、服饰、母语——那不是融入，那是抹除，而且它连效果都没有：它只会制造出一个心怀怨恨的第二代。

我想留给你的区分是：**强制*公民层面*的融入，绝不要强求*文化层面*的顺从。**你可以要求一个人学语言、守法律，但你不能要求他不再是他自己。真正做对的国家——大概像加拿大——恰恰因为要求的文化让步更少，反而融合得*更快*。

### 收尾金句 / Closing line
**English:** "Every one of these five questions comes back to the same move: stop asking *who belongs*, and start asking *what we're building together* — because diversity is not a problem to be managed, it's a condition of anything worth building."

**中文：**“这五个问题最后都回到同一个动作：别再问‘谁属于这里’，而开始问‘我们一起在建造什么’——因为多元不是一个需要被管理的问题，而是任何值得建造之物的前提条件。”
