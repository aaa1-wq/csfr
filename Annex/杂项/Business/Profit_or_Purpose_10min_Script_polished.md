# If I Lead a Business One Day — Profit or Purpose?
### A 10-minute speech in two opposing perspectives, with real examples · 中英对照（润色版）

> **How to deliver.** ~1,650 words, ≈10 minutes at a measured pace (140 wpm) with pauses. Structure: Opening → **Perspective A: Profit First** (Amazon, Ryanair) → **Perspective B: Purpose First** (Purdue, Wells Fargo, Patagonia, Novo Nordisk) → **The Cautionary Case: Enron** → **Synthesis** (Toyota, Whole Foods). `[ ]` = stage direction, do not read aloud. Bold lines are meant to land.
>
> 使用说明：英文在上、中文对照在下。`[ ]` 为舞台提示，不念出。加粗处放慢、加重。

## Opening (0:00–0:45)

If I lead a business one day — will I prioritize profit, or other goals?

It's easy to *answer* and hard to *mean*. So I won't give one clean answer; I'll give two honest ones. First I'll argue, as hard as I can, that I should put **profit first** — and mean it. Then I'll argue the opposite — **purpose first** — with real companies on both sides. Then I'll tell you which one I actually believe.

**中文：** 如果有一天我做企业——我会优先利润，还是其他目标？这个问题容易回答，却很难真正算数。所以我不给一个干净利落的答案，而是给两个诚实的。先尽全力论证我应该**利润优先**——而且我是认真的；再论证相反一方——**目标优先**——两边都用真实公司做例子。最后我告诉你我真正信哪一个。

---

## PERSPECTIVE A — Profit First (0:45–4:15)

Start with what profit is *not*: not greed, and not the enemy of purpose. **Profit is oxygen.** You can have all the purpose you want, but without oxygen you're just a beautiful idea that goes out of business.

**First, profit is the only thing that lets purpose survive time.** Amazon spent nearly a decade barely breaking even, plowing everything into growth and logistics — because Bezos understood you can't serve customers you can't reach, and can't scale what doesn't pay for itself. Had Amazon demanded a profit every quarter from day one, it would never have built the infrastructure to serve hundreds of millions of people cheaply. **Profit, or the path to it, is the permission to keep doing good.**

**Second, the market is a truth-teller, and profit is its language.** Ryanair is disliked by many of its own passengers — no frills, fees for everything — yet it is one of Europe's most profitable airlines, because it delivers the one thing customers actually chose it for: cheap, reliable flights across the continent. That's the market speaking. If I ignore profit to chase *my own* idea of "good," I may create value for no one but myself. **Profit keeps me honest about whether my purpose is real or just vanity.**

**Third — the uncomfortable truth: profit is what lets me compete at all.** A company that pays workers best and uses the cleanest materials carries a higher cost base. If it can't turn a profit, it loses to the cheaper rival who doesn't care — and when it dies, the people I meant to protect lose their jobs anyway. **A business that dies of its own virtue saves no one; it just hands the world to the less virtuous.** To do good at scale, I have to be profitable enough to stay in the game.

**中文：** 先说利润*不是*什么：不是贪婪，也不是目标的对立面。**利润是氧气。** 目标再多，没有氧气，你也只是一个破产的“美丽想法”。

**第一，利润是唯一能让目标活过时间的东西。** 亚马逊头将近十年几乎不盈利，把钱一直投回增长和物流——贝索斯明白，你服务不了够不着的客户，也扩大不了不自我造血的东西。如果亚马逊从第一天就要求每个季度都盈利，它永远建不起那套让几亿人低价购物的基础设施。**利润——或者说通向利润的路径——才是持续行善的许可。**

**第二，市场说真话，利润是它的语言。** 瑞安航空被不少自己的乘客嫌弃——没服务、样样收费——却是欧洲最赚钱的航空公司之一，因为它兑现了乘客真正选它的东西：便宜、准点、遍布欧洲的航班。这就是市场在说话。如果我为了追求*自己*定义的“好”而忽视利润，我可能只是在为自己一个人创造价值。**利润让我诚实面对：我的目标是真的，还是只是虚荣。**

**第三——一个不舒服的真相：利润才让我有资格竞争。** 给工人工资最高、用最干净材料的公司，成本更高。赚不到钱，它就会输给那个不在乎的、更便宜的对手——而它一旦倒闭，我本想保护的人照样失业。**一个死于自身美德的企业，谁都救不了，只是把世界交给更没德行的一方。** 要大规模行善，我必须足够盈利，才留在牌桌上。

## PERSPECTIVE B — Purpose First (4:15–7:45)

Now let me change my clothes — and show you why the profit-first case, for all its cleverness, is a trap.

The profit-first argument hides one assumption: that **profit measures value created.** It doesn't. Profit measures value *captured* — and the gap between those two words is where every corporate disaster lives.

**Look at Purdue Pharma.** OxyContin was a blockbuster; it made the Sackler family billions. By any quarterly measure, a spectacularly "successful" business. But profit is computed *after* you decide what to ignore — and what Purdue ignored was that the drug was addictive. Nearly 645,000 Americans died of opioid overdose between 1999 and 2021. The market eventually told the truth: Purdue went bankrupt, the family agreed to pay about $7.4 billion, and they were barred from selling opioids in the US. **Profit measured the money they captured, not the harm they created** — and it took two decades and hundreds of thousands of deaths to send the bill.

**Second problem: profit is a good servant and a terrible master.** Make profit the *goal* instead of the *gauge*, and people learn to game it. Wells Fargo's employees opened millions of fake customer accounts to hit sales targets — because the target became the goal, and hitting the number became the point. **Any metric that becomes the target stops being a good measure.**

**And the deepest point: if profit comes first, who holds me accountable — and how fast?** The market is *late*. It took Enron's collapse, the 2008 financial crisis, and Purdue's bankruptcy to price harms that ran for years. Purpose — an explicit commitment to the people my business touches — is what catches harm *before* the market does, before the lawsuit, before the grave.

Now the counter-evidence the profit side never mentions. **Patagonia**, an outdoor-clothing company, built its business explicitly around environmental purpose — and it isn't a charity: it has been profitable for decades. **Novo Nordisk** chose to prioritize insulin and obesity drugs over short-term margin and became Europe's most valuable company. Purpose didn't cost them shareholder value — it *created* it. **A company is not an engine for making money; it's a promise, and profit is how you keep it.**

**中文：** 现在我换身衣服，来告诉你为什么“利润优先”这套说法，无论多聪明，其实是个陷阱。

利润优先的论证藏着一个假设：**利润衡量的是被创造的价值。** 并不是。利润衡量的是被*攫取*的价值——“创造”和“攫取”之间的落差，正是所有企业灾难的所在。

**看看普渡制药。** 奥施康定是爆款，让萨克勒家族赚了几十亿。用任何季度的标准看，都是一门“极其成功”的生意。可利润是在*你决定忽略什么之后*才算出来的——而普渡忽略的，是这种药会上瘾。1999 到 2021 年，近 64.5 万美国人死于阿片过量。市场最终说了真话：普渡破产，家族同意赔付约 74 亿美元，并被禁止在美国销售阿片类药物。**利润衡量的是他们攫取的钱，不是他们造成的伤害**——而这张账单，等了两代人和几十万条人命才寄到。

**第二个问题：利润是好仆人，却是极糟的主人。** 把利润当成*目标*而不是*仪表*，人们就会去博弈它。富国银行的员工为了冲业绩指标，开了数百万个假客户账户——因为指标变成了目标，冲数字本身变成了意义。**任何一个指标，一旦变成目标，就不再是好指标。**

**最深的一点：如果利润优先，谁来问责我，又多快？** 市场是*迟到*的。安然崩塌、2008 金融危机、普渡破产，都是多年之后才给那些持续多年的伤害定价。而“目标”——对那些被我的企业触及的人所做的明确承诺——能在市场之前、诉讼之前、坟墓之前，抓住伤害。

再看利润方从不提的反证。**巴塔哥尼亚（Patagonia）**是一家户外服装公司，明确围绕环保目标做生意，而且不是慈善机构：几十年来一直盈利。**诺和诺德（Novo Nordisk）**选择优先研发胰岛素和减肥药，而不是短期利润率，结果成了欧洲市值最高的公司。目标没有葬送股东价值，反而*创造*了它。**一家公司不是赚钱的引擎，而是一个承诺，利润是你兑现承诺的方式。**

## THE CAUTIONARY CASE — What an "evil company" looks like (7:30–8:30)

If you want to see what profit-first looks like with the purpose stripped all the way out, look at **Enron**.

For years, Enron was the darling of Wall Street — named "America's Most Innovative Company" by *Fortune* six years running, its stock climbing while its executives told a story of endless growth. It was a lie. The growth was manufactured with accounting tricks and hidden debt, and when the truth came out in late 2001, the company collapsed in weeks. Thousands of employees lost their jobs *and* their retirement savings — because they had been encouraged to hold company stock. Investors lost around $74 billion. The executives who built the scheme had already cashed out. And the accounting firm, Arthur Andersen, was destroyed with it.

Here's why Enron belongs in this speech. Enron wasn't a company that merely *failed* to prioritize purpose — it was a company that made **deception itself the business model.** And it worked, quarter after quarter, right up until it didn't. That's the trap of profit-as-ultimate-goal in its purest form: when the number is the only thing that matters, lying about the number becomes a rational strategy — until the whole thing detonates, and the harm lands not on the people who built it, but on the ordinary workers and savers who trusted it.

An "evil company" doesn't have to be a cartoon villain. Enron's people were educated, polished, and certain they were the smartest in the room. **Evil, in business, rarely announces itself — it just optimizes.**

**中文：** 如果你想看看把目标彻底抽干之后、“利润优先”会变成什么样，看看**安然（Enron）**。

很多年里，安然是华尔街的宠儿——连续六年被《财富》评为“美国最具创新力的公司”，股价一路攀升，高管们讲着一个无限增长的故事。那是个谎言。所谓增长，是用会计手段和隐藏债务造出来的；2001 年底真相曝光，公司在几周内崩塌。数千名员工不仅失业，还失去了退休金——因为他们被鼓励持有公司股票。投资者损失约 740 亿美元。搭起这套骗局的高管早已套现离场，而审计方安达信（Arthur Andersen）也一并覆灭。

为什么安然要放进这场演讲？因为安然不是“没有优先考虑目标”的公司——它是**把欺骗本身做成了商业模式**的公司。而且它一个季度一个季度地奏效，直到彻底崩盘。这正是“把利润当终极目标”最纯粹的陷阱：当数字是唯一重要的事，谎报数字就成了一种理性策略——直到全盘引爆，而伤害并没有落在建造它的人身上，而是落在那些信任它的普通员工和储蓄者身上。

“邪恶公司”不一定长着卡通反派的脸。安然的人受过良好教育、仪表体面，还坚信自己是屋里最聪明的人。**在企业里，恶很少自报家门——它只是一味地优化。**

## SYNTHESIS — What I would actually do (8:30–10:00)

So which one? I've argued both hard enough to believe both. Now let me tell you what I'd actually do.

My honest position: **"profit or purpose" is a false choice — and that's exactly why it's dangerous.** People who pose it as a choice are usually selling one extreme. Profit-first people say purpose is naive; purpose-first people say profit is greed. Both want you to stop asking the harder question.

The harder question isn't *which* to prioritize — it's **how long is my horizon?**

If my horizon is one quarter, profit wins every time — and it *should*, because over one quarter, purpose is just a cost. But if my horizon is ten years, the calculus flips — because over ten years, trust compounds, employees stay, customers return, and the harm you externalized comes home. That's the lesson of **Toyota**: its "lean" philosophy looked wasteful in the short run — stopping the line the moment a defect appeared, spending on quality no quarterly report could see — yet over decades it became the world's most reliable carmaker *and* one of its most profitable. **In the long run, purpose becomes profitable, and profit without purpose destroys itself.**

So here's what I'd do. I'd **use profit as the instrument and purpose as the standard.** Profit tells me whether I'm healthy; purpose tells me whether I'm worth being healthy. And I'd build in **accountability by design** — not my own good intentions, which are worth little under pressure, but rules, disclosure, and people around me with the power to say "no." That's what Whole Foods tried to encode when it wrote its mission around stakeholder value — and the lesson from its later acquisition is that good intentions need institutional structure, not just a founder's conscience.

So my final answer: **I will prioritize profit — because I intend to still be here in ten years, and doing good is the only thing that compounds that long.**

Thank you.

**中文：** 那到底哪一个？我已经把两边论证到连自己都信。现在告诉你我真正会怎么做。

我的诚实立场是：**“利润还是目标”是个假二选一，而正因为它假，才危险。** 把一个复杂问题说成二选一的人，通常是在推销某个极端。利润优先的人说目标天真；目标优先的人说利润贪婪。两边都想让你别再问那个更难的问题。

更难的问题不是*优先哪个*，而是**我的时间尺度有多长？**

如果尺度是一个季度，利润每次都会赢，也应该赢——因为一个季度里，目标只是成本。可如果尺度是十年，算法就完全反转——十年里，信任会复利、员工会留下、客户会回头、你外部化的伤害会回家。这正是**丰田**的教训：它的“精益”哲学短期看像浪费——一发现缺陷就停线，为财报看不见的质量花钱——但几十年下来，它成了全球最可靠、也最赚钱的车企之一。**长期看，目标会变成绩效；失去目标的利润会摧毁自己。**

所以我会这么做：**把利润当工具，把目标当标准。** 利润告诉我健不健康；目标告诉我值不值得健康。我还要把**问责制度化**——不是靠自己的善意（压力面前，善意不值钱），而是规则、披露，以及身边那些有权说“不”的人。这正是全食超市（Whole Foods）当年围绕利益相关者价值写使命时想编码的东西——它后来被收购的教训是：好的意图需要制度结构，而不只是创始人的良心。

所以我的最终答案是：**我会优先利润——因为我打算十年后还活着，而做好事，是唯一能复利这么久的事。**

谢谢。
