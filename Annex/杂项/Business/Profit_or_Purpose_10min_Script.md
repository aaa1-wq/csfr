# If I Lead a Business One Day — Profit or Purpose?
### A 10-minute speech in two opposing perspectives, with real examples · 中英对照

> **How to deliver.** ~1,750 words, ≈10 minutes at a measured pace (140 wpm) with pauses. Structure: Opening → **Perspective A: Profit First** (real cases: Amazon, Ryanair) → **Perspective B: Purpose First** (real cases: Patagonia, Novo Nordisk, Purdue) → **The Cautionary Case: Enron** → **Synthesis** (real cases: Toyota, Whole Foods). `[ ]` = stage direction, do not read aloud. Bold lines are meant to land.
>
> 使用说明：英文在上、中文对照在下。`[ ]`为舞台提示，不念出。加粗处放慢、加重。

## Opening (0:00–0:45)

If I lead a business one day — will I prioritize profit, or other goals?

It's an easy question to *answer* and a very hard question to *mean*. So instead of giving you one clean answer, I'll give you two honest ones. First I'll argue, as forcefully as I can, that I should put **profit first** — and I'll mean it. Then I'll argue the opposite — **purpose first** — with real companies on both sides. Then I'll tell you which one I actually believe.

**中文：** 如果有一天我做企业——我会优先利润，还是其他目标？这个问题很容易*回答*，却很难*真正算数*。所以我不给你一个干净利落的答案，而给你两个诚实的答案。先尽全力论证我应该**利润优先**——而且我是认真的。然后再论证相反一方——**目标优先**——两边都用真实公司做例子。最后我告诉你我真正信哪一个。

---

## PERSPECTIVE A — Profit First (0:45–4:15)

Let me start with what profit is *not*: it is not greed, and it is not the enemy of purpose. **Profit is oxygen.** You can have all the purpose you want, but without oxygen, you're just a beautiful idea that goes out of business.

**First: profit is the only thing that lets purpose survive time.** Amazon spent nearly a decade barely breaking even — plowing everything back into growth and logistics — because Jeff Bezos understood that you cannot serve customers you can't reach and cannot afford to scale what doesn't pay for itself. If Amazon had insisted on being profitable every quarter from day one, it would never have built the infrastructure to serve hundreds of millions of people at low prices. **Profit, or the *path* to it, is the permission to keep doing good.**

**Second: the market is a truth-teller, and profit is its language.** Ryanair is disliked by many of its own passengers — no frills, fees for everything — and yet it is one of the most profitable airlines in Europe, because it delivers on the one thing its customers actually chose it for: cheap, reliable flights across the continent. That's the market speaking. If I ignore profit to pursue *my own* idea of "good," I may be creating value for nobody but myself. **Profit keeps me honest about whether my purpose is real or just vanity.**

**And third — the uncomfortable truth: profit is what lets me compete at all.** A company that pays workers best and uses the cleanest materials carries a higher cost base. If it can't turn a profit, it loses to the cheaper competitor who doesn't care — and when it dies, the people I wanted to protect lose their jobs anyway. **A business that dies of its own virtue doesn't save anyone; it hands the world to the less virtuous.** To do good at scale, I have to be profitable enough to stay in the game.

**中文：** 先说利润*不是*什么：它不是贪婪，也不是目标的对立面。**利润是氧气。** 你可以有再多目标，但没有氧气，你就只是一个破产了的“美丽想法”。

**第一：利润是唯一能让目标活过时间的东西。** 亚马逊在头将近十年几乎不盈利——把一切都投回增长和物流——因为贝索斯明白：你没法服务你够不着的客户，也没法扩大会自我造血之外的东西。如果亚马逊从第一天就坚持每季度都盈利，它永远建不起那套让几亿人低价购物的基础设施。**利润——或者说通往利润的路径——才是持续做善事的许可。**

**第二：市场是说真话的人，而利润是它的语言。** 瑞安航空被很多自己的乘客嫌弃——没服务、样样收费——可它是欧洲最赚钱的航空公司之一，因为它兑现了乘客真正选它的那种东西：便宜、准点、遍布欧洲的航班。这就是市场在说话。如果我为了追求*我自己*定义的“好”而忽视利润，我可能在为除了我自己以外的所有人创造价值。**利润让我诚实地面对：我的目标是真的，还是只是虚荣。**

**第三——不舒服的真相：利润才让我有资格竞争。** 一家给工人工资最高、用最干净材料的公司，成本更高。如果它赚不到钱，就会输给那个不在乎的、更便宜的对手——而当它倒闭时，我本想保护的人照样失业。**一个死于自身美德的企业，谁也没救下；它只是把世界交给了更没德行的那一方。** 要大规模地行善，我必须足够盈利、才能留在牌桌上。

## PERSPECTIVE B — Purpose First (4:15–7:45)

Now let me change my clothes. And I'll tell you why the profit-first case, for all its cleverness, is a trap.

The profit-first argument hides one assumption: that **profit measures value created.** It doesn't. Profit measures value *captured* — and the gap between those two words is where every corporate disaster lives.

**Look at Purdue Pharma.** OxyContin was a blockbuster — it made the Sackler family billions. By any quarterly measure, it was a spectacularly "successful" business. But profit is computed *after* you decide what to ignore — and what Purdue ignored was that the drug was addictive. Nearly 645,000 Americans died of opioid overdose from 1999 to 2021. The market eventually told the truth: Purdue went bankrupt, the family agreed to pay about $7.4 billion, and they were barred from selling opioids in the US. **Profit measured the money they captured, not the harm they created** — and it took two decades and hundreds of thousands of deaths to send the bill.

**Second problem: profit is a good servant and a terrible master.** Make profit the *goal* instead of the *gauge*, and people learn to game it. Wells Fargo's employees opened millions of fake customer accounts to hit sales targets — because the target became the goal, and hitting the number became the point. **Every metric that becomes the target stops being a good measure.**

**And the deepest point: if profit comes first, who holds me accountable — and how fast?** The market is *late*. It took Enron's collapse, the 2008 financial crisis, and Purdue's bankruptcy to price harms that ran for years. Purpose — an explicit commitment to the people my business touches — is what catches harm *before* the market does, before the lawsuit, before the grave.

Now the counter-evidence the profit side never mentions. **Patagonia** — an outdoor-clothing company — built a business explicitly around environmental purpose, and it's not a charity: it has been profitable for decades. **Novo Nordisk** chose to prioritize developing insulin and obesity drugs over short-term margin, and became Europe's most valuable company. Purpose didn't cost them shareholder value — it *created* it. **A company is not an engine for making money; it's a promise, and profit is how you keep it.**

**中文：** 现在我换身衣服。我来告诉你为什么“利润优先”这套说法——无论多聪明——其实是个陷阱。

利润优先的论证藏着一个假设：**利润衡量的是被创造的价值。** 并不是。利润衡量的是被*攫取*的价值——而“创造”和“攫取”这两个词之间的落差，正是所有企业灾难的所在。

**看看普渡制药。** 奥施康定是爆款——它让萨克勒家族赚了几十亿。用任何季度的标准看，它都是一门“极其成功”的生意。但利润是在*你决定忽略什么之后*才算出来的——而普渡忽略的，是这种药会成瘾。1999 到 2021 年，近 64.5 万美国人死于阿片过量。市场最终说了真话：普渡破产，家族同意赔付约 74 亿美元，并被禁止在美国销售阿片类药物。**利润衡量的是他们攫取的钱，而不是他们造成的伤害**——而账单是等了两代人、几十万条人命之后才寄到的。

**第二个问题：利润是好仆人，却是极糟的主人。** 把利润当作*目标*而不是*仪表*，人们就会去博弈它。富国银行的员工为了冲业绩指标，开了数百万个假客户账户——因为指标成了目标，冲数字本身成了意义。**任何一个指标一旦变成目标，就不再是一个好指标。**

**最深的一点：如果利润优先，谁来问责我——多快问责？** 市场是*迟到*的。安然崩塌、2008 金融危机、普渡破产——都是多年之后才给那些持续多年的伤害定价。而“目标”——对那些我的企业所触及的人做出的明确承诺——能在市场之前、在诉讼之前、在坟墓之前，抓住伤害。

再看利润方从不提的反证。**巴塔哥尼亚（Patagonia）**——一家户外服装公司——明确围绕环保目标来做生意，而且它不是慈善机构：几十年来一直盈利。**诺和诺德（Novo Nordisk）**选择优先研发胰岛素和减肥药，而非短期利润率，结果成了欧洲市值最高的公司。目标并没有葬送股东价值——它*创造*了股东价值。**一家公司不是赚钱的引擎，而是一个承诺，利润是你兑现这个承诺的方式。**

## THE CAUTIONARY CASE — What an "evil company" looks like (7:30–8:30)

If you want to see what profit-first looks like with the purpose stripped all the way out, look at **Enron**.

For years, Enron was the darling of Wall Street — named "America's Most Innovative Company" by *Fortune* six years running, its stock climbing while its executives told a story of endless growth. It was a lie. The growth was manufactured with accounting tricks and hidden debt, and when the truth came out in late 2001, the company collapsed in weeks. Thousands of employees lost their jobs *and* their retirement savings — because they had been encouraged to hold company stock. Investors lost around $74 billion. The executives who built the scheme had already cashed out. And the accounting firm, Arthur Andersen, was destroyed with it.

Here's why Enron belongs in this speech. Enron wasn't a company that merely *failed* to prioritize purpose — it was a company that made **deception itself the business model.** And it worked, quarter after quarter, right up until it didn't. That's the trap of profit-as-ultimate-goal in its purest form: when the number is the only thing that matters, lying about the number becomes a rational strategy — until the whole thing detonates, and the harm lands not on the people who built it, but on the ordinary workers and savers who trusted it.

An "evil company" doesn't have to be a cartoon villain. Enron's people were educated, polished, and certain they were the smartest in the room. **Evil, in business, rarely announces itself — it just optimizes.**

**中文：** 如果你想看看把目标彻底抽干之后、“利润优先”会变成什么样，看看**安然（Enron）**。很多年里，安然是华尔街的宠儿——连续六年被《财富》评为“美国最具创新力的公司”，股价一路攀升，高管们讲着一个无限增长的故事。那是个谎言。所谓增长，是用会计手段和隐藏债务造出来的；2001 年底真相曝光，公司在几周内崩塌。数千名员工不仅失业，还失去了退休金——因为他们被鼓励持有公司股票。投资者损失约 740 亿美元。搭起这套骗局的高管早已套现离场，而审计方安达信（Arthur Andersen）也一并覆灭。

为什么安然要放进这场演讲？因为安然不是“没有优先考虑目标”的公司——它是**把欺骗本身做成了商业模式**的公司。而且它一个季度一个季度地奏效，直到彻底崩盘。这正是“把利润当终极目标”最纯粹的陷阱：当数字是唯一重要的事，谎报数字就成了一种理性策略——直到全盘引爆，而伤害并没有落在建造它的人身上，而是落在那些信任它的普通员工和储蓄者身上。

“邪恶公司”不一定长着卡通反派的脸。安然的人受过良好教育、仪表体面，还坚信自己是屋里最聪明的人。**在企业里，恶很少自报家门——它只是一味地优化。**

## SYNTHESIS — What I would actually do (8:30–10:00)

So which one? I've argued both hard enough to believe both. Now let me tell you what I'd actually do.

My honest position: **"profit or purpose" is a false choice — and that's exactly why it's dangerous.** The people who pose it as a choice are usually selling one extreme. Profit-first people tell you purpose is naive; purpose-first people tell you profit is greed. Both want you to stop asking the harder question.

The harder question is not *which* to prioritize — it's **how long is my horizon?**

If my horizon is one quarter, profit wins every time, and it *should* — because at one quarter, purpose is just a cost. But if my horizon is ten years, the calculus flips — because over ten years, trust compounds, employees stay, customers return, and the harm you externalized comes home. That's exactly the lesson of **Toyota**: its "lean" philosophy looked wasteful in the short run — stopping the assembly line the moment a defect appeared, spending on quality nobody could see in a quarterly report — but over decades it became the world's most reliable carmaker *and* one of its most profitable. **Long-term, purpose becomes profitable, and profit without purpose destroys itself.**

So here's what I'd actually do. I'd **use profit as the instrument and purpose as the standard.** Profit tells me if I'm healthy; purpose tells me if I'm worth being healthy. And I'd build in **accountability by design** — not my own good intentions, which are worth little under pressure, but rules, disclosure, and people around me with the power to say "no." That's what Whole Foods tried to encode when it wrote its mission around stakeholder value — and the lesson from its later acquisition is that even good intentions need institutional structure, not just a founder's conscience.

So my final answer: **I will prioritize profit — because I intend to still be here in ten years, and doing good is the only thing that compounds that long.**

Thank you.

**中文：** 那到底哪一个？我已经把两边都论证到“连我自己都信”。现在告诉你我真正会怎么做。

我的诚实立场是：**“利润还是目标”是个假二选一——而正因为它假，它才危险。** 把一个复杂问题说成二选一的人，通常是在推销某一个极端。利润优先的人告诉你目标是天真的；目标优先的人告诉你利润是贪婪的。两边都想让你别再问那个更难的问题。

更难的问题不是*优先哪个*——而是**我的时间尺度有多长？**

如果我的尺度是一个季度，利润每次都赢，而且*理应*赢——因为在一个季度里，目标只是一种成本。但如果我的尺度是十年，算法就完全反转——因为十年里，信任会复利、员工会留下、客户会回头、你外部化的伤害会回家。这正是**丰田**的教训：它的“精益”哲学在短期看像是浪费——一发现缺陷就停线、为财报里看不见的质量花钱——但几十年下来，它成了全球最可靠、也最赚钱的车企之一。**长期看，目标会变成绩效；而失去目标的利润会摧毁自己。**

所以我会怎么做：把**利润当工具、把目标当标准。** 利润告诉我健不健康；目标告诉我值不值得健康。而且我要把**问责制度化**——不是我自己的善意（在压力面前善意不值钱），而是规则、披露，以及身边那些有权说“不”的人。这正是全食超市（Whole Foods）当年围绕利益相关者价值写使命时想编码的东西——而它后来被收购的教训是：好的意图需要制度结构，而不只是一个创始人的良心。

所以我的最终答案是：**我会优先利润——因为我打算十年后还活着，而做好事，是唯一能复利这么长久的事情。**

谢谢。
