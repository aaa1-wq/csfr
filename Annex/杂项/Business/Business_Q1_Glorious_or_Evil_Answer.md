# Q1: Are Businesses Glorious — or Evil?
### Answering the slide's first question · 同一行业、电影＋真实生活、一正一反 · 中英对照

> 题目 / The prompt: *Use examples from movies and real life to illustrate that 1) businesses can be glorious and promoting human progress, or 2) they are evil and wreaking havoc on the world. (It'd be ideal if these examples are from the same industry.)* — 用电影和真实生活的例子，说明企业可以辉煌、推动人类进步，或者邪恶、危害世界；最好来自同一行业。
>
> `[ ]` = stage direction, don't read aloud. Bold lines land — slow down. 英文在上，中文在下。

---

## Opening — Why "same industry" is the smart part (0:00–0:40)

The prompt says it'd be "ideal if these examples are from the same industry" — and that one clause is the whole argument. Because if you pick a good business from one industry and an evil one from another, people just say, "Well, that industry is bad." The moment you pick **the same industry**, you take the industry off the hook — and the question becomes: **what makes one business a savior and the other a destroyer, when they live in the very same world?** That's the real question. I'll answer it in one industry: **pharmaceuticals / chemicals.**

**中文：** 题目说，例子“最好来自同一行业”——而这半句话就是全部论点所在。如果从 A 行业挑一个善、从 B 行业挑一个恶，人们只会说“那个行业本来就坏”。可一旦你挑**同一个行业**，行业本身就被排除在外了，问题于是变成：**在同一个世界里，为什么一家企业成了救星、另一家成了毁灭者？**这才是真问题。我用一个行业来回答：**医药／化工。**

---

## The Glorious Side — Movies & Real Life (0:40–2:20)

**Real life: Jonas Salk and the polio vaccine.** In 1955, Salk's vaccine was proven to work against polio — a disease that paralyzed half a million people a year, most of them children. When asked who owned the patent, Salk famously said: **"There is no patent. Could you patent the sun?"** Pharma here isn't a predator; it's humanity's best-funded problem-solver. Smallpox was eradicated. Polio is nearly gone. And the more recent, harder case: **mRNA vaccines** compressed roughly a decade of vaccine R&D into about a year during COVID-19 — an achievement that saved an estimated millions of lives, and that only a profit-driven company with massive R&D capacity could have pulled off at that speed.

**Movie: *The Imitation Game* (2014).** Alan Turing's team at Bletchley Park wasn't a corporation, but it dramatizes the *spirit* we celebrate in glorious enterprise — brilliant people applying technology to shorten a war and save lives. (For a pure business film on the good side, *The Founder* dramatizes McDonald's system — the idea that a great operating system, scaled, can feed billions cheaply.)

So the "glorious" case is not sentimentality: **private R&D, scale, and the profit motive are what let a medicine reach billions of people fast.** Profit is the engine that funded the cure.

**中文：** **真实案例：索尔克与小儿麻痹症疫苗。**1955 年，索尔克的疫苗被证实能对抗小儿麻痹——一种每年让约 50 万人瘫痪、多数是儿童的疾病。被问及专利归谁时，索尔克有句名言：**“没有专利。你能给太阳申请专利吗？”**这里，医药不是掠夺者，而是人类**资金最充裕的问题解决者**。天花被消灭，小儿麻痹几近绝迹。更新的硬例子：**mRNA 疫苗**在新冠期间把约十年的疫苗研发压缩到一年——估计挽救了数百万生命，而只有一家由利润驱动、拥有庞大研发能力的公司才可能在那种速度下做到。

**电影：《模仿游戏》(2014)。**图灵团队并非企业，但它把我们在“辉煌企业”身上赞颂的那种精神戏剧化了——聪明人用技术缩短战争、拯救生命。（若你要纯商业的正面片，《大创业家》/ *The Founder* 讲麦当劳的体系：一套伟大的运营系统被规模化，就能便宜地喂饱亿万人的故事。）

所以“辉煌”绝非矫情：**私营研发、规模、利润动机，正是让一种药快速抵达数十亿人的原因。**利润是给解药供能的那台引擎。

## The Evil Side — Same Industry, Movies & Real Life (2:20–4:20)

Now flip the coin — **the same industry, the same century.**

**Movie: *Painkiller* (2023).** It dramatizes Purdue Pharma and OxyContin — a "blockbuster" built on two moves: claiming only about 0.3% of users would get addicted (a figure with no real basis), and sending sales reps to push doctors to prescribe and increase dosages. By any quarterly measure, it was a "successful" business.

**Real life — the exact same thing happened.** Purdue's OxyContin fueled the US opioid crisis: **nearly 645,000 Americans died of opioid overdose between 1999 and 2021**, and in a 2025 settlement the Sackler family agreed to pay billions and was barred from selling opioids in the US. Here's the deeper point: **profit is computed *after* you decide what to ignore.** What Purdue ignored was that the drug was addictive. Profit measured the money they *captured*, not the harm they *created*.

And the court-proven case: **the tobacco industry** — chemicals and a consumer product in one. Same logic: it made a lethal, addictive product, denied it for decades, and in 2006 a US court found the major companies **guilty of racketeering** for conspiring to defraud the public about the addictiveness of nicotine.

So here's what the same-industry comparison exposes: **it's not the industry that decides good or evil. It's a choice — whether a business uses its power to solve human problems or to extract from human weakness.**

**中文：** 现在翻硬币——**同一个行业，同一个世纪。**

**电影：《止痛骗》（*Painkiller*, 2023）。**它讲的是普渡制药和奥施康定——一个靠两招造出来的“爆款”：声称只有约 0.3% 的使用者会上瘾（这个数字毫无可靠依据），以及派销售代表游说医生开方、加大剂量。用任何季度的标准看，它都是“成功”的生意。

**真实案例——完全一样的事真的发生了。**普渡的奥施康定点燃了美国阿片危机：**1999 到 2021 年，近 64.5 万美国人死于阿片过量**；2025 年的和解中，萨克勒家族同意赔付数十亿、并被禁止在美国销售阿片类药物。更深的点：**利润是在你决定忽略什么之后才算出来的。**普渡忽略的，是这种药会上瘾。利润衡量的是他们*攫取*的钱，而不是*造成*的伤害。

更硬、法院判过的案例：**烟草业**——既是化工也是消费品。同样的逻辑：它生产致命且成瘾的产品，几十年来一直否认，2006 年美国法院判定几家大公司因合谋欺骗公众尼古丁成瘾性而**犯有诈骗罪（racketeering）**。

所以这“同一行业”的对照揭示的是：**决定善恶的不是行业，而是一种选择——企业是把自己的力量用来解决人类的问题，还是用来从人性的弱点里榨取。**

---

## The Turn — So Where Do *We* Fit? (4:20–5:30)

But I want to add the part most answers miss. If an evil business survives, it's not because it's strong — it's because **we keep buying.** The tobacco industry killed about half its long-term users and still sells; Purdue sold OxyContin for over a decade because patients asked for it. So the answer to "are businesses good or evil" has a third party: **the consumer.** Every dollar we spend is a vote — and unlike a real election, we cast it dozens of times a week.

That's actually hopeful. It means the same-industry comparison isn't a verdict we're stuck with — it's a **choice we hand back to businesses through demand.** The most powerful ESG policy in the world isn't a regulation or a mission statement; it's a buyer willing to pay for their beliefs — and keep walking away.

**中文：** 但我想补上多数回答漏掉的一环。如果邪恶企业能活下来，不是因为它们强大，而是因为**我们一直在买。**烟草业杀死了约一半的长期使用者，却仍在卖；普渡卖了十多年奥施康定，因为病人点名要。所以“企业是善是恶”这个问题还有第三方：**消费者。**我们花的每一块钱都是一票——而且跟真正的选举不同，这张票我们一周要投几十次。

这其实是好事。它意味着“同一行业”的对照不是一个我们只能接受的结果，而是**我们通过需求交还给企业的一个选择。**世界上最有力量的 ESG 政策，不是法规、也不是使命宣言，而是一个愿意为自己的信念买单、并且一直转身离开的消费者。

---

## Close — One Sentence (5:30–6:00)

So: are businesses glorious or evil? **They're an engine — and a glorious engine can drive an ambulance or a tank.** The industry doesn't decide which. **The people who run it decide — and the people who buy it decide too.** That's why, in the same industry and the same century, one company saves millions and another kills them: not because of "business," but because of what we point it at — and what we pay for.

**中文：** 那么：企业是辉煌还是邪恶？**它是一台引擎——而一台辉煌的引擎，可以开成救护车，也可以开成坦克。**决定方向的不是行业，**而是经营它的人——以及购买它的人。**这就是为什么在同一个行业、同一个世纪里，一家公司救活数百万人，另一家却害死他们：不是因为“商业”，而是因为我们把它指向了什么——以及我们为它付了多少钱。
